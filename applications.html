TECHNICAL MASHING GUIDE

The Cereal Cooker
Mashing Schedule.

Because corn starch has a higher gelatinization threshold (68°C–74°C) than barley malt enzymes can tolerate, grits are processed through a cereal cooker prior to main mash integration:

1
Pre-Mash In & Malt Addition

Blend maize grits into brewing liquor (ratio ~1:3.5) with 10–15% crushed barley malt. The malt's alpha-amylase prevents the slurry from thickening into an un-pumpable paste.

2
Liquefaction Rest (70°C – 74°C)

Hold for 15–20 minutes. Starch granules swell, absorb water, and rupture, enabling alpha-amylase to rapidly cleave long amylose and amylopectin chains.

3
Boil & Mash-Tun Transfer (100°C)

Boil the cooker mash for 15–30 minutes to complete gelatinization, then pump directly into the main malt mash to raise the combined bed to saccharification temperature (~65°C).

The Science of Foam Retention

Beer head stability depends upon hydrophobic proteins from malt cross-linking with isomerized hop alpha-acids. However, free lipids (corn oils) are powerful foam-destroying surfactants that collapse bubbles instantly.

Why Godawari Grits Protect Your Foam:

Our multi-stage mechanical degermers strip corn oil down to < 0.95% (dry basis). This ultra-low lipid profile guarantees your beer pours with a dense, creamy foam head and tight Belgian lacing.
